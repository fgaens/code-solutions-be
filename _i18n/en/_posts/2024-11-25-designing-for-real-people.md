A well-designed website or app is about more than aesthetics. It creates an experience in which users feel confident, satisfied and in control. By understanding how users interact with your platform, you can identify areas for improvement, from simplifying navigation to refining calls to action.

That is why, ever since my studies in Product Design, I have been a strong advocate of universal design. This approach focuses on creating products, environments and services that are accessible and usable for as many people as possible, regardless of age, ability or background. With this principle in mind, testing becomes a vital step in the development of a new website: not only with individual users, but also with groups, to gather diverse perspectives.

Start by sitting down with individual users and guiding them through specific tasks. Observing their actions shows you how they navigate through the application. It also lets you ask targeted questions in real time and uncover pain points, preferences and moments of confusion.

For a broader perspective, bring groups of users together to work with a website or app. Focus groups encourage discussion and produce insights that might not arise in one-to-one sessions. This setting gives a well-rounded view of how users with different perspectives engage with a platform.

Beyond tracking clicks or time spent on a page, pay close attention to user behaviour. Are users frustrated, confused or pleasantly surprised? Their expressions reveal emotional responses that data alone cannot capture. How do they move the cursor? Do they scroll endlessly, hover over certain areas or repeatedly click back and forth? These actions point to potential usability problems.

From our perspective, user tests are an essential step in the web design process. They ensure that the final product is not only functional but also intuitive and user-friendly. Skipping this step means risking that you overlook insights that determine the quality of the user experience. By prioritising user testing, you create a website that meets the needs of its audience and builds trust and engagement.

Simply put, user testing is not just a helpful step. It is a fundamental part of designing for real people.
